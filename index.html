The Null Ledger

Two systematic stock-picking models, built and then tested to destruction with a pre-registered, independently audited process. The published answer for both: no real edge after costs. This site keeps testing that answer — forward, on paper, in public.

Registered expectation: null. Nothing here is a strategy to follow. Both models are expected not to beat their benchmarks after trading costs — that is the pre-registered, audited conclusion. This page exists so anyone (including the author) can watch that conclusion face new data every month. If a line runs hot for a while, that is what randomness looks like; the project's own published rules say years of forward months decide, not streaks.

Model 1 — the S&P 500 ranker

A monthly machine-learning system over S&P 500 stocks: momentum and price-history features, walk-forward XGBoost (no future data in training), top ~30 names, conviction-weighted, 10% position cap, 30% sector cap, rebalanced monthly against real trading costs.

What testing found: on a survivorship-honest rebuild of its data — including the stocks that later died and left the index — the backtest's apparent +3.1%/yr alpha collapsed to −0.4%/yr (t = −0.15): statistically nothing. The exciting version of this model was an illusion created by testing on today's surviving stocks. It paper-trades on anyway, because a published null deserves a live test too.

Model 2 — the same machine, aimed at small-caps (“SMALL/clone”)

The identical production machinery cloned — zero re-tuning — into the small-cap band (dollar-volume ranks 1001–2000), where market folklore says edges hide. It was one of 18 pre-registered strategies tested in a gated campaign: hypotheses frozen before any result existed, a build sample (2009–2018) gated by 2,000 cost-bearing placebo portfolios, and a confirm sample (2019–2026) that could be touched exactly once, ever.

What happened: it was the campaign's sole build-sample survivor (+1.1%/yr, placebo-calibrated p = 0.0135) — then the one-shot confirm test rejected it on every registered prong. The chart below is the actual sealed confirm window, plotted from the published artifacts.

Current positioning — what the models actually hold

Both models' holdings come from locked monthly snapshots — files written the moment picks are made and never edited, so the positions provably predate their returns. Buys and sells are the month-over-month changes between snapshots.

Every registered strategy, disclosed

Selective reporting is how backtests lie: run twenty ideas, publish the winner. The defense is simple — register everything first, then publish everything. Below is the complete pre-registered slate and each cell's build-sample result under the frozen rules, including the embarrassing rows. Net and gross are annualized excess vs the cell's band benchmark; p is the placebo-calibrated rank against 2,000 cost-bearing imposters (gate: ≤ 0.023).

Disqualified = the cell's placebo null failed a registered validity check (delisting-exposure mismatch) in the anti-conservative direction, so by pre-stated rule it cannot pass — its small p is exactly the false positive the check predicted. The gross column is the campaign's real finding: information exists; costs eat it.

Why these nulls can be trusted

Anyone can publish a backtest. The value here is the process: every hypothesis, threshold, and consequence was written down and hash-frozen before results existed; every deviation went through a signed, dated amendment protocol; three independent audit sessions reproduced every binding number bit-for-bit from pinned artifacts; and the final test ran once, under seal, with the null distribution locked before the answer was revealed. The full record — pre-registration, 42 amendments, 16 verbatim sign-offs, audit reports, hash manifests — is preserved.

The campaign's one genuinely useful market finding: several signals do carry real predictive information — six beat their placebo nulls at p-values as low as 0.0005 — and trading costs consume all of it. The information is real; the profit is not. That is the oldest result in the anomalies literature, reproduced independently on delisting-honest data.

Educational research project by a student. Paper trading only — no real money is or was traded, and nothing here is investment advice. Ledgers are append-only; locked monthly snapshots prove picks predate returns; missed months appear as gaps, never backfills.
