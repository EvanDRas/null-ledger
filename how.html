How it all works

Everything on this site, explained in plain language: what the two models actually do, how they were tested, and why the live record can be trusted.

Model 1 — the S&P 500 ranker, step by step

  1. Rank. At each month-end, every S&P 500 stock is scored on 13 price/volume features (momentum, trend quality, volatility, range position) by a gradient-boosted model, blended 50/50 with plain 6-month momentum. The model is trained walk-forward: to make the January 2020 pick it trains only on data available before January 2020 — it can never peek at the future, and it can only pick from stocks that were actually in the index on the decision date, including companies that later went bankrupt.
  2. Select & weight. The top ~30 names are held, higher scores get more money, capped at 10% per stock and 30% per sector.
  3. Repeat. One month later, re-rank everything. Turnover limits keep changes to ~7–8 stocks a month, and every trade is charged 15 basis points per side in simulated costs. No human decisions anywhere.

Model 2 — SMALL/clone: the same machine, moved down-market

The identical production code — imported, not copied, so re-tuning was structurally impossible — was pointed at the small-cap band: stocks ranked 1001–2000 by dollar volume, rebuilt point-in-time each month from a 13,821-ticker archive that includes every delisted name. Two honesty rules matter most:

  • Delisting-honest returns. When a held stock dies (bankruptcy, buyout, delisting), its final fate is in the return — and its exit is charged trading costs. Most retail backtests silently skip both, which is where fake small-cap edges usually come from.
  • Realistic small-cap costs. 75 basis points per side in the small band (40 in mid), plus a registered "haircut" stress that assumes costs are even worse. Small stocks are expensive to trade; pretending otherwise is the other place fake edges come from.

How the testing worked (and why "null" is the headline)

1 · Everything was written down first. Before any test ran, a frozen pre-registration fixed the 18 candidate strategies, every threshold, and every consequence — then the document's cryptographic hash was recorded so it provably never changed. No moving the goalposts after seeing results.

2 · Each strategy had to beat 2,000 imposters. Instead of asking "did it make money?" (almost anything does in a rising market), each strategy was compared against 2,000 placebo versions of itself: portfolios built by the same engine, paying the same costs, but trading on deliberately scrambled information. A real edge has to beat nearly all of its own imposters. This is the strictest practical test for "is there actual information here, or just luck and market exposure?"

3 · Two exams, and the final one could be taken once, ever. Strategies were built and gated on 2009–2018 data. The 2019–2026 data was sealed — touched exactly one time, for the single survivor, with the pass bar and the null distribution locked and hash-logged before the result was revealed. No retries, no tweaking, published either way.

4 · Independent audits. Three separate audit sessions, with no stake in the outcome, reproduced every binding number bit-for-bit from the frozen artifacts — and found real bugs, which went through a signed, dated amendment protocol before any fix. The full trail (42 amendments, 16 sign-offs) is preserved.

The result: six strategies showed statistically real predictive information — and trading costs consumed every basis point of it. The one build-sample survivor failed its one-shot confirm test on every prong. No edge survived. That's the published answer, and the reason this site promises nothing.

How the live paper trading proves itself

  • Automated. On the 1st of each month a scheduled job updates prices, retrains, rebalances, and scores the previous month. No manual input, so there's nothing to fudge.
  • Picks are locked before returns exist. Each month's portfolio is written to a timestamped snapshot file the moment it's chosen. When the month ends, the snapshot is scored against what actually happened. The snapshot's existence before the returns is what makes the record evidence rather than a story.
  • Append-only. The ledger only ever gains rows. A missed month shows as a gap — never a backfilled guess. Costs are charged on every simulated trade.
  • Paper. No real money is involved anywhere. The point is the answer, not the P&L.

How this site updates

After each monthly run, a build script re-reads the ledgers and published artifacts, regenerates the front page's data, and copies the latest dashboard and account pages here. The site never computes anything itself — it only displays the records the systems produce. If a number here ever disagreed with the underlying ledger files, the ledger files win.

Small glossary

Alpha
Return above what the benchmark delivered — the part a strategy can take credit for. Quoted per year.
Placebo test / empirical p
Beat 2,000 information-scrambled imposters of yourself. The p-value is your rank among them: p = 0.0135 means only ~1.3% of imposters did better. Lower is stronger.
Walk-forward
The model is always trained only on data from before the decision it's making — the way real life works.
Turnover
How much of the portfolio changes each month. Every unit of it costs money, which is the villain of this entire project.
Paper trading
Running the strategy with simulated money against real market prices, under real rules. All results here are paper.
Null result
The finding that there is no effect — here, no edge after costs. Publishing nulls honestly is rare and is the entire point of this site.
